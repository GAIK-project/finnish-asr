Teaching Whisper to hear Finnish dialects

A speech-to-text model trained on open dialect interviews recorded in the 1960s and 70s. This page explains what data we used, what we did with it, where Claude Opus 5.5 came in, and how the result was measured.

The whole pipeline at a glance

Audio

SKN dialect interviews from 50 municipalities, cut into 2–25 s segments.

Opus: punctuation

Opus added full stops, commas and capitals to the training text. The words were not allowed to change.

Training

A LoRA fine-tune of Whisper large-v3 on LUMI, keeping the best of three epochs.

WER tests

A dialect test from 10 municipalities the model never heard, plus standard-Finnish FLEURS and VoxPopuli.

Opus: judge

On a second dialect corpus, Opus blindly counted the meaning errors in every model's transcript.

The steps in brown are the two places where the published model used a language model. Opus never heard any audio and never wrote a test reference.

01

Data: where the audio and the texts came from

Suomen kielen näytteitä (SKN), "Samples of Spoken Finnish", is a corpus of the Institute for the Languages of Finland (Kotus), distributed by the Language Bank of Finland (Kielipankki) under CC BY 4.0. It holds about two hours of interviews from each of 50 municipalities across Finland, mostly with elderly speakers. Every sentence is transcribed twice: in dialect spelling (with phonetic marks) and in standard Finnish word forms.

We cut the interviews into 2–25 second segments and split them by municipality: 38 municipalities for training (15 042 segments, 62 h), 2 for validation (Pälkäne, Sotkamo) and 10 for the test, every fifth number of the SKN booklet series. The test municipalities represent five of SKN's six main dialect groups: south-western and Häme dialects, Savo and the south-east. The Ostrobothnian and Peräpohjola dialects are in training only. The model never heard any interviewee from a test municipality; a few interviewers recorded both test and training municipalities, and their questions are kept in the segments.

In English: "Do you still know how? Yes, I'll know it as long as I live."

What does llm-punctuation mean in the dataset? It says that the punctuation and capitals in the row's text field were added by Opus. The words are still the person's: an answer was accepted only if its words were exactly those of the human standard-Finnish transcript. The value human means text is the human text as it is: that holds for the 18 training segments whose punctuation failed the check, and for the whole test split.

Why punctuation? SKN's standard-Finnish text has none, so the first version of the model learned to write almost without full stops or commas: 1 of its 600 test outputs had one, against 97.5 % for Whisper large-v3.

02

Where Claude Opus 5.5 was used

Opus had four jobs. The first two concern the published model, the third only an internal comparison model, and the fourth the scoring of a webinar clip. On top of that, the training and LUMI code was written with Claude Code.

Punctuation for the training text

Opus got 20 standard-Finnish texts at a time (text only, no audio) and added punctuation and capitals. The code rejected every answer whose words differed from the original.

15 024 / 15 042 accepted · about USD 52 · prepare_targets.py

Blind judge on LA-murre

LA-murre's reference is in dialect spelling, so word error rate would punish standard Finnish. Opus read the reference and four models' transcripts, unnamed, and counted only meaning errors.

583 segments · about USD 11 per round · judge_dialect.py

Dialect to standard Finnish (internal model only)

In an extra experiment Opus rewrote the dialect-spelling texts of LA-murre's training recordings in standard Finnish, word for word. The model trained on them is the best on both dialect tests, but LA-murre's licence forbids sharing a derived version, so it was not published.

15 529 segments · about USD 49 · not published

Judges for the webinar clip

Transcripts of a 180 s dental webinar were scored by Opus and Gemini 3.1 Pro: each read the human text and one transcript without the model's name, counted meaning errors and checked 14 names and terms.

all 20+ set-ups · judge_asr.py

Opus cost about USD 125 for the training text and the first two judging rounds; a third round, for the timestamp variant (section 08), used about as many tokens as each of them. Every test reference was made by people, and no model output was ever used as the right answer.

03

Training on LUMI

The base is OpenAI's Whisper large-v3 (1.55 billion parameters), the best open model for ordinary Finnish in our comparison. The whole model was not retrained. LoRA adds small extra matrices to the attention layers and trains only those: about 2 % of the weights. At the end the add-on was merged into the weights, so the published model works like any Whisper model.

  • 25 250 training segments: SKN 15 042, FLEURS 2 704, VoxPopuli 7 504.
  • LoRA rank 64 on the attention q_proj and v_proj layers, learning rate 1e-4, batch 8, three passes (epochs) over the data.
  • Run: one AMD MI250X GPU on CSC's LUMI supercomputer, 3 h 8 min. The model was chosen on the validation municipalities, never on test results.
04

Measure 1: word error rate (WER)

WER counts how many words must be substituted, deleted or inserted to turn the model's text into the human text, divided by the number of words in the human text. Before comparing, text is lower-cased and punctuation removed. On the dialect test the reference is SKN's standard-Finnish transcript: the model is right when it hears dialect and writes standard Finnish.

05

Measure 2: an LLM judge on a second dialect corpus

The SKN test shares SKN's own writing conventions with the training data, so we needed a second, completely unseen corpus: LA-murre, 583 segments from ten localities that are not in SKN. Its reference, however, is written in dialect spelling, so WER would punish a model for exactly what we want from it: writing standard Finnish.

So for every segment Opus read the dialect reference and the transcripts of four models, labelled A–D and in a different order each time. It counted only errors that change the meaning: a wrong word, name or number, missing or invented content. Dialect against standard forms, punctuation and filler words are not errors.

The judge's instructions, word for word 
You compare automatic transcripts of Finnish dialect speech with a human reference.

The reference is a linguist's transcript in DIALECT spelling (for example "miekii" = "minäkin",
"viej juomaakii" = "vien juomaakin", "irvistie" = "irvistää"). The transcripts should
say the same thing; writing it in standard Finnish is correct and wanted.

For each transcript count only MEANING errors against the reference:
- a wrong word, name or number that changes what is said
- content that is missing
- content that was not said
Severity: critical = the segment's main point is lost or reversed; major = a content word,
name or number is wrong or a clause is missing; minor = a small slip a reader can see through.

NOT errors: dialect against standard forms, digits against number words, punctuation, case,
compound spacing, dropped fillers or repetitions ("niin niin", "tuota"), a cut-off word left out.
Judge each transcript on its own; the labels carry no information.

This is the public version, dialect-judge-v2. The scores above were made with v1, which differs only in the three example word pairs of the first paragraph: v1's came from the LA-murre corpus, these come from SKN.

Limitation: the judge is itself a language model; three open models judged the same task again (next section). Differences of one point per 100 words are read as ties unless a sign test says otherwise. The gap between the published model and Whisper is still clear: it had fewer points in 398 segments and more in 89 (sign test p < 0.001). The gap to the round-1 model is not significant (p = 0.10).

07

A human-checked reference for the webinar clip

The webinar clip's reference transcript came from the University of Helsinki team, and it looked like a corrected WhisperX draft, which would favour Whisper-family output. On 14 October 2026 the developer listened to the 42 spots where most systems heard something else and corrected 14 of them: mostly spoken forms that had been written in standard Finnish, two missing words and a brand name.

Against the checked text the leading systems sit within about one point of each other: gpt-4o-mini-transcribe 13.2, Whisper large-v3 13.7, Gemini 3.5 Flash 13.7, our published model 14.0 and ElevenLabs 14.2 % WER. The WhisperX-based set-ups lose their lead (10.4 → 14.0 and 11.9 → 15.7 %). A clip of about 400 words cannot separate systems that close.

08

The timestamp variant

Whisper normally writes time marks as it transcribes, and uses them to keep its place in long audio. The published model was trained on text without them, so its times are unusable: only 1 % of SKN's sentence starts get a time mark within one second (Whisper large-v3: 61 %). That is why its model card cuts long audio into pieces.

A second run of the same recipe trained half of the SKN segments with time marks taken from SKN's own sentence times (15 040 of the 15 042 had usable times). It puts 58 % of the sentence starts within one second (median 0.04 s when it finds one) and keeps the dialect accuracy.

When to use it. When you need sentence times, or want to give it long audio without cutting it yourself: load the timestamps revision of the same Hugging Face model and always use OpenAI's temperature fallback, which decodes a stretch again when its text starts to repeat (the model card has the settings). Without the fallback it looped in 3 of the 10 interviews (25.2 % WER).

It did not replace the published model. The rule set before the run allowed SKN to get at most about 0.5 points worse and FLEURS and VoxPopuli no worse; VoxPopuli got 0.4 points worse, and the webinar in pieces was also 0.5–1.3 points worse. On LA-murre the blind judge could not tell the two apart (fewer points in 98 segments, more in 100).

09

Where the model is weaker

  • Rare names and terms in standard-Finnish speech. On the webinar the judges found more name and term errors than for plain Whisper (for example "Nobelbayer" for Nobel Biocare), although its WER was lower. For standard Finnish full of brand names, plain Whisper is the safer choice.
  • Old speech, not every dialect. Both dialect corpora are interviews with elderly speakers from the 1960s and 70s. Today's regional speech was not tested, and the SKN test has no Ostrobothnian dialect.
  • Standard Finnish, not a phonetic dialect transcript. The model writes what is said, in standard Finnish word forms.
  • Long audio must be cut. Whisper's own long-form loop made the model repeat one phrase. Pieces of 15–25 s cut at quiet points work (13.7 % WER on the webinar); the model card has the code. For sentence times, or long audio in one go, use the timestamp variant with the fallback settings (section 08).
  • One training run per model. The numbers come from a single seed; the main judge was cross-checked by three open models (section 06).
